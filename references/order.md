# Order

Nobody arrives at the front door. They arrive wherever the link they clicked went, and that link was chosen by a search engine, an algorithm, or whoever pasted it.

The homepage is the entrance in exactly one scenario: somebody typed the domain. For everyone else, first contact is a search result, a social card, a profile, a third-party article, or a page four levels deep that happens to rank for a phrase nobody targeted.

This means the most carefully maintained surface is often not the one doing the work, and the one doing the work is often not maintained at all.

## The finding people are not ready for

Run the search. Write down the first ten results in order. Then ask the user which one they would have chosen as the first thing a stranger sees.

It is almost never the first result, and frequently it is not in the top ten at all.

That gap is the entire Order axis. A strong body of work can score badly here, because scoring badly on Order does not mean the work is weak. It means the order is not the one they would pick, and they have never looked.

## How to run it

Search the way a stranger would, which is not how the user would.

- **Their name alone.** For a person this is the single most common lookup, usually before a meeting or an interview
- **Their name plus the category.** "<name> marketing", "<name> consultant"
- **The company name alone**
- **The company plus the category**
- **The thing they want to be known for**, in the words an outsider would use rather than theirs

That last one is the most useful and the most ignored. If someone has spent two years building a position, searching that position should find them. Often it finds nothing of theirs at all, which is a coverage failure wearing an order costume.

Record the first ten for the most relevant two or three queries. Note which are owned, claimed or third-party, and note anything that is not them, because results about someone with the same name are part of what a stranger sees.

Use more than one engine where practical, and note that results vary by location, history and time. Record the date. This is a snapshot, not a constant, and reporting it as a constant is a mistake.

## Reading it

**Position one is a different thing from positions two through ten.** The first result absorbs most of the attention. Whatever it is, it is the answer to the question for most people.

**Third-party results above owned results** is the common shape and it is not automatically bad. A credible outlet ranking above a personal site can be a strength. It becomes a problem when the third-party page is stale, wrong, or about work they have moved on from.

**Nothing of theirs on the first page** for the category they want to own is the finding worth leading with. No amount of new work fixes it if the new work goes somewhere that does not rank.

**A dormant profile outranking a live one** is the most common single result worth acting on, because it is cheap to fix and nobody has looked.

## The front door

After recording the order, name the surface that should be first contact. One. Then compare it to what is actually first.

Three outcomes:

1. They match. Score Order high and move on
2. The intended front door is on the page but not first. Usually fixable by strengthening it and linking to it consistently
3. The intended front door is absent. The problem is not order, it is that the page is not findable, and that is a build problem rather than an audit finding

Be careful with recommendations here. This skill can identify the gap honestly. Closing it is search and content work with its own long feedback loop, and promising a specific ranking outcome is not something this or anything else can honestly do.

## The mode

**First ten** is this, alone, with no recommendations attached. Search, list, stop.

It works because it is uncomfortable and because it takes ten minutes. Most people have never seen their own results written down as a list, and the list argues for itself without anybody editorializing over it.
